The purpose of the inquiry

The Four Noble Truths

UnderstandAbandonRealizeDevelop

The Four Noble Truths frame the inquiry into suffering and release. Dependent arising examines the conditions involved. Each truth carries a task; open one below to connect study with practice.

01 Suffering — understand

Dukkha. Birth, aging, death, separation, and frustrated wishes belong to the scope of suffering; the discourse summarizes it in the five clinging-aggregates.

In the model: the sequence ends with aging-and-death and the suffering named in the formula.

Daily inquiry: What is difficult here? Distinguish the event, feeling tone, and your interpretation. Understanding starts with attending honestly to experience.

02 Origin — abandon

Samudaya. The discourse identifies craving leading to renewed existence: craving for sensual pleasure, becoming, and non-becoming.

In the model: craving belongs within a wider account of conditions, including ignorance, feeling, and clinging.

Daily inquiry: What am I demanding, pursuing, or holding tightly? Consider external circumstances too. The task concerns abandoning craving, with care for ordinary needs and appropriate action.

03 Cessation — realize

Nirodha. Cessation is the complete fading and relinquishment of that craving.

In the model: the cessation view follows the ending of the sustaining conditions. Sensory experience continues in an awakened living person.

Daily inquiry: What changes when I stop feeding this particular demand? Temporary easing is something to investigate; full cessation is a deeper claim than a passing moment of calm.

04 Path — develop

Magga. The path is the Noble Eightfold Path: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration.

In the model: understanding conditions belongs with ethical conduct and cultivation of the mind.

Daily inquiry: What response can I cultivate now? Clarify an assumption, speak carefully, set an appropriate boundary, or repair harm.

The Upanisā sequence describes supporting conditions for liberation. It complements the Eightfold Path while retaining its distinct list of factors.

Definitions and tasks: SN 56.11 · Setting the Wheel of Dhamma in Motion ↗, translated by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu; passages defining the four truths and their respective tasks. Summaries here are paraphrases. Daily inquiries are educational applications, rather than quotations or attainment tests.

Bodhi, Connected Discourses, vol. I, pp. 522–524; SN 12.43. For the Eightfold Path, SN 12.65 ↗; for the companion sequence, SN 12.23 ↗.

Primary discourse · translated by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu

Start with the definitions of the four truths and the task associated with each: understanding, abandoning, realizing, and developing. This is the main source for this page.

MN 141 · An Analysis of the Truths

Primary discourse · translated by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu

Sāriputta explains the truths in greater detail, including the forms of suffering and each factor of the Eightfold Path. Read this when a brief definition raises further questions.

Explore the books · chapters to begin with

Rupert Gethin · The Foundations of Buddhism

Academic introduction · Oxford University Press, 1998

Begin: Chapter 3, “Four Truths: The Disease, the Cause, the Cure, the Medicine,” pp. 59–84.

A structured introduction organized around suffering, its origin, nirvāṇa, and the path. A useful first book for understanding how the four truths fit together.

Publisher · book information ↗

Peter Harvey · An Introduction to Buddhism

Academic introduction · Cambridge University Press, 2nd edition, 2013

Begin: Chapter 3, “Early Buddhist Teachings: The Four True Realities for the Spiritually Ennobled,” pp. 50–87. For the connection with dependent arising, focus on pp. 65–72.

Helpful for terminology and the relationship between the four truths, conditionality, and the wider Buddhist path. Harvey’s chapter title reflects his translation approach.

Publisher · book information ↗

Bhikkhu Bodhi · In the Buddha’s Words

Translated discourse anthology · Wisdom Publications, 2005

Begin: Chapter IX, “Shining the Light of Wisdom,” especially the selections on the Four Noble Truths.

A route into further primary texts, with introductions from a Theravāda perspective. Useful for studying the truths alongside dependent origination and other teachings on wisdom.
